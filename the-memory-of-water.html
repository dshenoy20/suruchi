Book Chapter

The Memory of Water: Ritual Immersion, Riverbanks, and Ecological Memory in Chhath Pooja

AuthorAanchal, Saumya
InChhath Puja: Multidisciplinary Perspectives on Faith, Culture and Community
PublisherIndian National Trust for Art and Cultural Heritage (INTACH)
DateApril 2026

Abstract

This paper explores how waterbodies—rivers, ponds, and ritual ghats—become sites of memory in the practice of Chhath Pooja, one of the most significant festivals in Bihar and eastern Uttar Pradesh. While often seen mainly as a devotional act of offering gratitude to the Sun, Chhath is also a ritual of remembering: remembering ancestors, community ties, and an ecological bond with water. Drawing on Jan Assmann's idea of cultural memory and Paul Connerton's work on commemorative ritual, the paper argues that the immersion of devotees and the offerings made to the setting and rising Sun inscribe both ecological and ancestral memory onto these waterscapes.

The discussion focuses on three dimensions. First, ritual space as mnemonic space: how ghats turn into sacred landscapes where gestures of worship and collective gatherings reanimate cultural memory. Second, ecological memory: rivers and ponds carry not only sacred value but also environmental histories, from ideas of purity to today's concerns of pollution, encroachment, and disappearing ghats. Third, migration and the remapping of memory: in cities across India and the diaspora, where natural riverbanks may be absent, artificial ponds and urban waterscapes stand in for ancestral rivers, carrying forward memory into new geographies.

By bringing together ritual, ecology, and cultural transmission, the paper highlights Chhath as more than a religious observance: it is a practice through which communities sustain ecological awareness and cultural resilience. At a time when ecological degradation threatens both waterbodies and cultural continuity, Chhath emerges as a ritual archive that preserves memory through embodied devotion, communal gathering, and ecological imagination.
