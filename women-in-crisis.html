Book Chapter · Forthcoming

Women in Crisis / Women as Crisis: The Gendered Aesthetics of Mithila Art During Covid-19

AuthorsAnushree, A. & Aanchal, S.
PublisherCambridge Scholars Publishing
DateDecember 2026 (forthcoming)

Abstract

The COVID-19 pandemic reproduced a longstanding paradox in Indian patriarchal discourse that women are simultaneously constructed as crisis — symbols of social instability whose conduct must be regulated — and positioned in crisis, as caregivers whose sustaining labor remains structurally invisible. This paper examines how Mithila paintings created by women in Bihar during the 2020–21 lockdowns challenge this double bind, transforming the dominant masculine vocabulary of pandemic emergency into a visual language of care, continuity, and collective world-making. Drawing on Hannah Arendt's conceptual triad of labor, work, and action, the paper offers close readings of selected works from the virtual exhibition Mithila Art in the Times of Covid (Syracuse University Art Museum, 2020). It argues that these women artists move from invisible domestic labor to Arendtian action — public gestures of appearing and speaking that resist the 'worldlessness' of lockdown. In doing so, Mithila painting emerges not merely as aesthetic documentation but as a vernacular feminist politics, a collective practice that redefines crisis not as rupture alone, but as an occasion for relational reconstruction and renewal.
